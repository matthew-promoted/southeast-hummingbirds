Hang the first feeder before the earliest birds reach your region. Ruby-throated Hummingbirds move north on a schedule set by day length, and the birds that cross the Gulf have just flown hundreds of miles without stopping. A clean feeder is an easy first meal.

## Spring arrival

Put feeders out a little ahead of the typical first sighting, not the day you see a bird. Across the Southeast that window runs from mid-February in South Florida to early April in the mountains:

- **South Florida:** mid-February
- **Central Florida and the south Georgia / south Alabama coast:** late February to early March
- **North Florida and the South Carolina coast:** early March
- **Piedmont (central Georgia, central Alabama, inland South Carolina):** mid to late March
- **North Georgia and north Alabama mountains:** late March to early April

Dates shift with weather and elevation. The [migration timing guide](/migration) has the regional table, including peak season and fall departure.

## What to put in the feeder

Use plain white sugar and water at **4 parts water to 1 part sugar**. Skip red dye, honey, brown sugar, and artificial sweeteners. The [nectar recipe](/nectar) covers the ratio, storage, and how often to change it when the weather turns hot.

## Fall and the last birds

Migration south is also driven by day length. Leaving a feeder up does not trap birds in your yard. Keep nectar available for about two weeks after your last sighting so stragglers can refuel. Most of Georgia, Alabama, and South Carolina is quiet by mid to late October. South Florida can still host birds later, and a few spend the winter.

If a hummingbird shows up after the Ruby-throated birds have gone, it may be a rare western visitor. The [species guide](/species) covers Rufous, Black-chinned, and Calliope hummingbirds.
